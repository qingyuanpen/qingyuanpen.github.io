RECORD AT A GLANCE

  • The material was sheep large-intestine mucus. This update now covers the stage status of all four groups, A through D.
  • This is a redacted public record. Reagent formulas, exact quantities, temperatures, speeds, durations, operating sequence, and storage locations are omitted.
  • Group B freeze-drying measurements and moisture/dry-matter calculations are complete, with no procedural anomaly recorded; replicate-level data remain internal.
  • External liquid ingress changed the composition of one group C replicate, so it can no longer serve as a valid parallel sample.
  • Group D first browned and later contained black particles. The cause is unconfirmed; stopping the group does not prove that the candidate process itself failed.
  • Solution leakage affected part of group A, introduced an unknown loss, and makes those samples unsuitable for direct formal quantitative comparison.
  • The complete timeline, operating conditions, and sample movements remain in the internal experimental record and Research OS.

How do the deviations affect interpretation?

DEV-A · SAMPLE LOSS

Solution leakage affected part of group A

Impact: an unknown amount of lipid and solvent may have been lost, changing the final concentration. Disposition: retain the record and assess validity or repeat before formal quantification.

QC-B · WITHIN-BATCH VARIATION

Group B quality signal, not a procedural anomaly

Impact: same-batch technical replicates show visible variation. Disposition: retain every value; verify mixing, aliquoting, and constant-mass criteria in the next run.

DEV-C · CONTAMINATION

External liquid entered one group C sample

Impact: system composition, buffering state, and contamination risk became uncontrolled. Disposition: stop downstream work and exclude the affected sample from valid group C parallels.

DEV-D · APPEARANCE

Group D browned during processing

Impact: oxidation, degradation, or another change is possible, but color alone cannot identify the mechanism. Disposition: record the observation without presenting a cause as established fact.

DEV-D · PARTICULATE

Black particles in group D

Impact: sample-, container-, environment-, and reagent-related contamination remain unresolved. Disposition: stop the group and exclude it from a valid C/D process comparison.

What can be concluded now?

CURRENT DECISION

This batch cannot support a valid comparison between the two candidate processes.

Although the group B baseline is complete, one group C replicate lost comparability through external liquid ingress, while the entire group D stopped because of browning and black particles. The record supports the conclusion that critical process deviations disrupted this comparison—not that either candidate process itself caused failure.

Group A has reached lipid reconstitution, but leakage in part of the group still requires a separate validity assessment. Within-group variation in group B also remains visible as a quality signal. Keeping these facts explicit prevents affected measurements from later being treated as normal replicates.

Open record items and next steps

  • Complete group A colorimetric quantification. Resolve the validity of leak-affected samples before completing the standard curve, readings, and unit conversion.
  • Verify group B within-batch variation. Predefine constant-mass criteria and strengthen pre-aliquot mixing and weighing records in the next run.
  • Continue the group C endpoint record. Capture the end state and disposition of the unaffected samples after the second extraction.
  • Complete sample disposition. Record each sample's current container, storage location, closure, and final disposition.
  • Link image evidence. If photographs exist, associate them with the undispersed material, D-group browning, black particles, and leakage traces.
  • Re-establish a paired C/D comparison. A later comparison should use matched samples from the same batch under pre-locked, consistently maintained conditions.
