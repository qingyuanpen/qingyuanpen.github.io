Group A | Lipid reconstitution complete, with leakage in part of the group

  1. A · STAGE
    The lipid reconstitution stage is complete

    Group A has completed the current lipid-extraction preparation and reconstitution. Colorimetric readings and quantitative conversion are next.

  2. A · PILOT
    The first single-tube plate-reader pilot is complete

    Standard response increased overall, and the identities of two adjacent standards were corrected in the internal record. The independent QC missed its target substantially and the sample signal exceeded the standard range, so this run is retained only for troubleshooting and selecting the next conditions, not formal quantitation.

  3. A · VALIDITY
    Two recent color-development trials were invalid

    Liquid-handling deviations made the reaction systems inconsistent, and a corrective aspiration may also have removed standard material. Raw readings remain in the internal record, but neither batch is calculated or reported as a formal quantitative result.

  4. A · 01
    Small leaks affected part of group A

    The leaks occurred at the lipid reconstitution-solution stage. The lost volume was not quantified, so the remaining concentration cannot be assumed to match the planned value.

  5. RECORD
    Logged as a sample-loss deviation

    The affected samples remain in the deviation record but should not support formal quantitative conclusions until validity is assessed or the samples are remade.

  6. A · 02
    Group A retained under the designated storage condition

    The storage condition, containers, closures, and exact location remain in the internal record only.

Group B | Moisture and dry-matter baseline complete

Wet-sample, freeze-dried, and repeat freeze-dried measurements are complete, together with moisture and dry-matter calculations. No procedural anomaly was reported. Replicate-level masses, formulas, and summaries remain in the internal page and Research OS.

  1. B · 01
    The freeze-drying measurement chain is complete

    Container, wet-sample, first freeze-dried, and repeat freeze-dried masses were recorded, making both derived percentages traceable.

  2. B · QC

    The variation is not being relabeled as an experimental anomaly, and no value is being deleted. A later run should predefine constant-mass criteria and improve pre-aliquot mixing.

The three group B technical replicates came from the same pooled sample. They describe within-batch variation, not three independent biological replicates.

Group D | Browning, black foreign material, and termination

Group D represents candidate process two. After the first processing and centrifugation stage, both the liquid phase and the collected supernatant appeared distinctly yellow-brown and turbid. Black foreign material was observed after the second extraction medium was added, and the group then stopped. Its composition and source remain unidentified.

  1. D · 01

    A visible color change was recorded as an appearance deviation.

  2. D · 02

  3. D · STOP
    Black foreign material observed, then processing stopped

    No further processing was performed. Browning, turbidity, and black material remain qualitative appearance records; composition and mechanism have not been established.

  4. D · RESTART
    New source material has a separate restart record

    The new material is numbered and tracked separately from the earlier anomalous batch. Exact mass, aliquoting plan, and operating schedule are not disclosed publicly.

How do the deviations affect interpretation?

DEV-A · SAMPLE LOSS

Solution leakage affected part of group A

Impact: an unknown amount of lipid and solvent may have been lost, changing the final concentration. Disposition: retain the record and assess validity or repeat before formal quantification.

DEV-A · COLOR DEVELOPMENT

Liquid-handling deviations affected two recent color-development trials

Impact: reaction systems were inconsistent, and corrective aspiration may have removed standard material. Disposition: mark both batches invalid, retain the raw record for review, and report no quantitative result.

QC-B · WITHIN-BATCH VARIATION

Group B quality signal, not a procedural anomaly

Impact: same-batch technical replicates show visible variation. Disposition: retain every value; verify mixing, aliquoting, and constant-mass criteria in the next run.

DEV-C · CONTAMINATION

External liquid entered one group C sample

Impact: system composition, buffering state, and contamination risk became uncontrolled. Disposition: stop downstream work and exclude the affected sample from valid group C parallels.

RECORD · CORRECTION

Withdraw the incorrect group C dark-particle statement

Impact: the wrong attribution entered both the public page and internal ledger. Disposition: prioritize the user's on-site confirmation—no unidentified dark particles were observed in group C; black material belongs only to the earlier group D record.

DEV-D · APPEARANCE

Group D browned, remained turbid, and contained black material

Impact: the material may have changed or foreign material may be present, but appearance alone cannot identify the mechanism. Disposition: retain the facts and keep the group stopped without presenting a cause as established.

What can be concluded now?

CURRENT DECISION

This batch cannot support a valid comparison between the two candidate processes.

Although the group B baseline is complete, one group C replicate lost comparability through external liquid ingress and the remaining samples were processed in split intervals across several calendar days. Group D browned, produced a turbid yellow-brown supernatant, contained black foreign material, and then stopped. The record shows that process deviations disrupted the comparison—not that either process itself caused failure.

No quantitative result is reported from the first group A plate-reader pilot or from the two subsequent color-development trials: the first failed QC and range review, while the later two had liquid-handling deviations. The internal record retains all raw readings, deviations, and decisions. A later batch should begin only after the tube map, operator calls, and critical additions are locked and verified. Leak-affected samples remain exploratory and will not be pooled with unaffected samples. Within-group variation in group B also remains visible as a quality signal.

Open record items and next steps

  • Complete the pre-run review for the next group A batch. Lock the tube map, operator calls, critical-addition verification, standards, and independent QC. Report a result only if the entire batch passes predefined validity criteria. Keep leak-affected samples exploratory.
  • Verify group B within-batch variation. Predefine constant-mass criteria and strengthen pre-aliquot mixing and weighing records in the next run.
  • Continue the next-stage group C record. Preserve the excluded status of the affected replicate, and add endpoint appearance, downstream disposition, and sample location for the remaining samples.
  • Track the group D restart separately. Open a new batch record for the new source material; keep the earlier anomalous group D batch stopped and do not merge the records.
  • Complete sample disposition. Record each sample's current container, storage location, closure, and final disposition.
  • Maintain the image-evidence trail. New images should be linked on the same day to stage, group, source filename, and interpretation boundary.
  • Re-establish a paired C/D comparison. A later comparison should use matched samples from the same batch under pre-locked, consistently maintained conditions.
