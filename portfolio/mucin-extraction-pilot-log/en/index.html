RECORD AT A GLANCE

  • The material was sheep large-intestine mucus. This update now covers the stage status of all four groups, A through D.
  • This is a redacted public record. Reagent formulas, exact quantities, temperatures, speeds, durations, operating sequence, and storage locations are omitted.
  • Group B freeze-drying measurements and moisture/dry-matter calculations are complete, with no procedural anomaly recorded; replicate-level data remain internal.
  • External liquid ingress changed the composition of one group C replicate, so it can no longer serve as a valid parallel sample.
  • A 29 July record correction confirms that no unidentified dark particles were observed in group C; the earlier group C particle statement was inaccurate.
  • Group D browned, produced a more turbid yellow-brown supernatant, and contained black foreign material; its composition and source remain unidentified.
  • The remaining group C samples resumed ice-bath stirring on 27 July after split processing and interim cold storage; the endpoint remains unreported.

Group C | Continued processing, sample exclusion, and record correction

Incomplete dispersion was observed during this round. In a later stage, external liquid entered one replicate and eliminated its comparability. The remaining samples completed a split processing period, entered interim cold storage, and resumed ice-bath stirring on 27 July. On 29 July, the user explicitly corrected the record: no unidentified dark particles were observed in group C.

  1. C · 01

  2. C · 02

    Subsequent operations and storage followed the experimental record. Their order and exact conditions are not disclosed publicly.

  3. C · 03

  4. C · 04
    Ice-bath stirring resumed on 27 July

    The remaining samples restarted processing. They were still in progress at the time of this update, so endpoint appearance, end time, and subsequent disposition remain unreported.

  5. C · STOP
    External liquid ingress

    The affected sample's composition and contamination risk became uncontrolled. It was logged as a deviation sample and removed from further work.

  6. C · CORRECTION
    Dark-particle statement withdrawn

    The user confirmed that no unidentified dark particles were observed in group C. The earlier photo-based group attribution was inaccurate and has now been corrected in both the public page and internal Research OS.

The confirmed group C deviation is external liquid ingress into one replicate. The statement that unidentified dark particles were present in group C was an error and is no longer used as a fact or interpretation basis.

Group D | Browning, black foreign material, and termination

Group D represents candidate process two. After the first processing and centrifugation stage, both the liquid phase and the collected supernatant appeared distinctly yellow-brown and turbid. Black foreign material was observed after the second extraction medium was added, and the group then stopped. Its composition and source remain unidentified.

  1. D · 01
    Browning during processing

    A visible color change was recorded as an appearance deviation.

  2. D · 02
    The post-centrifugation supernatant remained yellow-brown and turbid

    Same-stage images show a darker and more turbid appearance than group C. Lighting and capture conditions limit this to a qualitative comparison.

  3. D · STOP
    Black foreign material observed, then processing stopped

    No further processing was performed. Browning, turbidity, and black material remain qualitative appearance records; composition and mechanism have not been established.

How do the deviations affect interpretation?

DEV-A · SAMPLE LOSS

Solution leakage affected part of group A

Impact: an unknown amount of lipid and solvent may have been lost, changing the final concentration. Disposition: retain the record and assess validity or repeat before formal quantification.

QC-B · WITHIN-BATCH VARIATION

Group B quality signal, not a procedural anomaly

Impact: same-batch technical replicates show visible variation. Disposition: retain every value; verify mixing, aliquoting, and constant-mass criteria in the next run.

DEV-C · CONTAMINATION

External liquid entered one group C sample

Impact: system composition, buffering state, and contamination risk became uncontrolled. Disposition: stop downstream work and exclude the affected sample from valid group C parallels.

RECORD · CORRECTION

Group C dark-particle statement withdrawn

Impact: the wrong attribution entered both public and internal records. Disposition: prioritize the user’s field confirmation, state that no unidentified dark particles were observed in group C, and retain the correction date.

DEV-D · APPEARANCE

Group D browned, remained turbid, and contained black material

Impact: the material may have changed or foreign material may be present, but appearance alone cannot identify the mechanism. Disposition: retain the facts and keep the group stopped without presenting a cause as established.

What can be concluded now?

CURRENT DECISION

This batch cannot support a valid comparison between the two candidate processes.

Although the group B baseline is complete, one group C replicate lost comparability through external liquid ingress and the remaining samples were processed in split intervals across several calendar days. Group D browned, produced a turbid yellow-brown supernatant, contained black foreign material, and then stopped. The record shows that process deviations disrupted the comparison—not that either process itself caused failure.

Group A has reached lipid reconstitution, and the macro-scale color-development arithmetic and batch totals have been checked, but no absorbance or lipid result is available yet. Reagent identity, blank provenance, independent QC, cuvette compatibility, and per-tube timing must be locked before the run. Leak-affected samples remain exploratory and will not be pooled with unaffected samples. Within-group variation in group B also remains visible as a quality signal.

Open record items and next steps

  • Close the group A pre-run gates. Lock reagent identity, blank provenance, independent QC, cuvette compatibility, and reading cadence before standards, QC, blanks, and samples are processed in the same analytical batch. Keep leak-affected samples exploratory.
  • Verify group B within-batch variation. Predefine constant-mass criteria and strengthen pre-aliquot mixing and weighing records in the next run.
  • Complete the group C endpoint record. Add the end time, total duration, endpoint appearance, and sample disposition after the 27 July restart.
  • Keep the corrected attribution consistent. Do not reintroduce unobserved dark particles into group C; retain group D black material only as a qualitative observation until composition and source are resolved.
  • Reconcile why group D stopped. Separate the confirmed browning/turbidity observations from the still-missing on-site stopping rationale.
  • Complete sample disposition. Record each sample's current container, storage location, closure, and final disposition.
  • Maintain the image-evidence trail. New images should be linked on the same day to stage, group, source filename, and interpretation boundary.
  • Re-establish a paired C/D comparison. A later comparison should use matched samples from the same batch under pre-locked, consistently maintained conditions.
