RECORD AT A GLANCE

  • The material was sheep large-intestine mucus. This update covers groups A, C, and D only; group B is outside the present update.
  • This is a redacted public version. Exact centrifugation, buffer-addition, stirring, and storage parameters remain in the internal record.
  • Ice-water ingress changed the volume and composition of C2, so it can no longer serve as a valid parallel sample in group C.
  • Group D first browned and later contained black particles. The cause is unconfirmed; stopping the group does not prove that the no-acetone process itself failed.
  • Leakage from A1/A2 introduced an unknown loss and makes these samples unsuitable for direct formal quantitative comparison.
  • This round of C/D processing was dated 24 July. After overnight cold storage, C1/C3 resumed ice-bath stirring at 11:00 on 25 July.

Experimental groups and the scope of this update

The four-group design addresses two levels of the study. Groups A/B establish lipid and dry-matter baselines for the original material; groups C/D compare PBS extraction with or without acetone defatting. This page updates only the groups for which new information was provided and does not fill unknown group B details.

Group C | PBS extraction progress and ice-water ingress into C2

Group C is the comparator that retains acetone defatting. Large undispersed material remained after the first PBS stirring step. The samples then underwent cold storage, centrifugation, renewed PBS addition, and a second ice-bath stirring step. During that second step, C2 experienced major volume contamination.

    Large undispersed pieces remained, indicating that the material had not formed a uniform dispersion.

  2. C · 02
    Held under low-temperature conditions

    The incompletely dispersed material was transferred to low-temperature storage. Exact temperature and duration are not disclosed in this public version.

  3. 24 JUL 2026
    Centrifugation and renewed PBS addition

    The supernatant was collected and PBS was added to each sample. Centrifugation and buffer-addition parameters remain in the internal record.

  4. 24 JUL 2026
    Second ice-bath stirring stage

    This step continued dispersion and extraction in PBS. After the stage was completed, C1/C3 were transferred to low-temperature storage overnight.

  5. 24 JUL 2026 · C2 STOP
    Substantial ice water entered C2

    After the ice bath melted, tipping allowed ice water to enter C2. The sample was then classified as abnormal, transferred to low-temperature storage, and removed from downstream work.

  6. 25 JUL 2026 · 11:00
    C1/C3 resumed ice-bath stirring

    Stirring continued after overnight low-temperature storage. This entry records the start of the operation only; its end time and later disposition remain open.

The C2 problem was not simply “a little extra water.” System volume, PBS concentration, contamination risk, and extraction conditions all became uncontrolled, eliminating comparability with C1/C3.

Group D | Browning, black particles, and termination

Group D was designed to observe PBS extraction after removing the acetone step. The samples browned after the first prolonged room-temperature stirring period. Black particles were then found during later processing after cold storage. Because sample integrity and the source of the particles could not be established, no further work was performed.

  1. D · 01
    First prolonged PBS stirring stage at room temperature

    The samples appeared brown at the end of stirring, which was logged as a visible abnormal change.

  2. D · 02

  3. 24 JUL 2026

  4. 24 JUL 2026 · STOP
    Black particles were observed

    The source was not identified. Group D was returned to low-temperature storage and no further experimental processing was performed.

How do the deviations affect interpretation?

DEV-A · SAMPLE LOSS

A1/A2 reconstitution-solution leakage

Impact: an unknown amount of lipid and solvent may have been lost, changing the final concentration. Disposition: retain the record and assess validity or repeat before formal quantification.

DEV-C2 · CONTAMINATION

Substantial ice water entered C2

Impact: volume, ionic strength, buffering, and contamination risk became uncontrolled. Disposition: stop downstream work and exclude C2 from valid group C parallels.

DEV-D1 · APPEARANCE

Group D browned after stirring

Impact: oxidation, degradation, or another change is possible, but color alone cannot identify the mechanism. Disposition: record the observation without presenting a cause as established fact.

DEV-D2 · PARTICULATE

Black particles in group D

Impact: sample-, container-, environment-, and reagent-related contamination remain unresolved. Disposition: stop the group and exclude it from a valid C/D process comparison.

What can be concluded now?

CURRENT DECISION

This batch cannot yet answer whether acetone can be removed.

C2 lost comparability through ice-water ingress, while the entire group D was stopped because of browning and black particles. The record supports the conclusion that critical process deviations disrupted this comparison—not that the no-acetone process itself caused failure.

Likewise, the A1/A2 leaks mean that the lipid-baseline data require separate validity assessment. Keeping these deviations visible prevents affected measurements from later being treated as normal replicates and creates evidence for improving stirring, temperature control, and sample-transfer procedures.

Open record items and next steps

  • Continue recovering exact timing. The C/D processing date is confirmed as 24 July, and C1/C3 restarted stirring at 11:00 on 25 July. Exact start/end times for earlier steps continue to be reconciled in the internal record.
  • Complete sample disposition. Record the current containers, storage locations, closures, and final disposition of C1/C3, C2, and group D.
  • Link image evidence. If photographs exist, associate them with the undispersed material, D-group browning, black particles, and leakage traces.
  • Resolve group A validity. State whether A1/A2 are invalidated or repeated and whether A3 remained intact.
  • Re-establish a paired C/D comparison. A later comparison should use fresh parallel samples from the same batch with matched temperature, stirring time, containers, and protection against ice-water ingress.
